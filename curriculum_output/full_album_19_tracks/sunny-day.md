## 1. 🎯 Quest Mission (1 Sentence)
Join Vesta & Silas in Terrasol on an acoustic sensory quest to explore Sunny Day.

---

## 2. 🥁 Step 1: The Rhythm Ignition (2 Minutes)
Teacher models a steady 2-minute rhythmic call-and-response beat to 'Sunny Day', synchronizing clapping and vocal chanting with the children.

---

## 3. 🖐️ Step 2: Tactile Discovery Ritual (7 Minutes)
The Hands-On Sensory Discovery:
1. Children gather in a circle with tactile items.
2. Follow the musical cues of Sunny Day to touch, sort, and move in rhythmic unison.
3. Pair high-reach movements with deep somatic exhalations.
4. Freeze in quiet stillness at the final chord.

---

## 4. 💬 Step 3: Seriphia's Socratic Wonder Circle (4 Minutes)
> **Rhythm Cue:** Three-Beat Detective Knock (90 BPM: Knock-Knock-Knock — 'What Clue Did We Notice?')  
> **Wait-Time Rule:** Hold a calm 4-beat pause after every question before inviting responses.  
> **Critical Thinking Strategies Activated:** `S-5`, `S-30`, `S-32`, `S-33` | **Intellectual Standards:** Accuracy, Specificity, Clarity

1. Intellectual Humility (S-5): Before stepping outside on a sunny morning, how can we check whether the air is warm or chilly instead of just guessing from bright light?
2. Testing Assumptions (S-30): Many people assume bright sunshine always means hot weather—what sensory evidence (skin, breath, frost, wind) proves that assumption isn't always true?
3. Plausible Inferences (S-32): If we see long shadows stretching across the rug or yard, what clues tell us where the Sun is in the sky right now?

### Developmental Band Scaffolds (Ages 2–7)
- **Sprouts (Ages 2–4):** Point to warm sunlit spots vs. cool shady spots and say 'I notice...' before guessing.
- **Pathfinders (Ages 4–5):** Touch two stones—one from the sun and one from the shade—to compare direct temperature clues.
- **Wayfarers (Ages 6–7):** Trace why winter sunlight can look bright while air stays cold, testing assumption vs. observation.
