## 2. 🥁 Step 1: The Rhythm Ignition (2 Minutes)
Teacher models a steady 2-minute rhythmic call-and-response beat to 'One Hundred', synchronizing clapping and vocal chanting with the children.

---

## 3. 🖐️ Step 2: Tactile Discovery Ritual (7 Minutes)
The Hands-On Sensory Discovery:
1. Children gather in a circle with tactile items.
2. Follow the musical cues of One Hundred to touch, sort, and move in rhythmic unison.
3. Pair high-reach movements with deep somatic exhalations.
4. Freeze in quiet stillness at the final chord.

---

## 4. 💬 Step 3: Seriphia's Socratic Wonder Circle (4 Minutes)
> **Rhythm Cue:** 100 BPM Decuple Clap-Check (10-20-30... Spot the Structure!)  
> **Wait-Time Rule:** Hold a calm 4-beat pause after every question before inviting responses.  
> **Critical Thinking Strategies Activated:** `S-10`, `S-15`, `S-29`, `S-31`, `S-34` | **Intellectual Standards:** Precision, Logic, Completeness

1. Noting Structural Similarities & Differences (S-29): How is counting by tens (10, 20, 30... 100) like counting 1 to 10—and what makes ten-bundles faster and more reliable?
2. Developing Evaluation Criteria (S-15): If we have a giant jar of 100 beans, what criteria make a counting method fair and easy to double-check without losing track?
3. Refining Generalizations (S-10): Does a bigger pile ALWAYS have more items than a neat stack of 100 small stones? How could big blocks trick our eyes?

### Developmental Band Scaffolds (Ages 2–7)
- **Sprouts (Ages 2–4):** Drop 10 stones into a cup together and cheer each full bundle of ten.
- **Pathfinders (Ages 4–5):** Compare a loose messy pile of 20 beans with two neat 10-stone rows to check accuracy.
- **Wayfarers (Ages 6–7):** Design a rule for verifying a 100-item count and explain why visual volume can mislead.
