---

## 2. 🥁 Step 1: The Rhythm Ignition (2 Minutes)
Teacher models a steady 2-minute rhythmic call-and-response beat to 'Horses Interlude', synchronizing clapping and vocal chanting with the children.

---

## 3. 🖐️ Step 2: Tactile Discovery Ritual (7 Minutes)
The Hands-On Sensory Discovery:
1. Children gather in a circle with tactile items.
2. Follow the musical cues of Horses Interlude to touch, sort, and move in rhythmic unison.
3. Pair high-reach movements with deep somatic exhalations.
4. Freeze in quiet stillness at the final chord.

---

## 4. 💬 Step 3: Seriphia's Socratic Wonder Circle (4 Minutes)
> **Rhythm Cue:** Three-Beat Detective Knock ('Wait a Minute — Check the Evidence!')  
> **Wait-Time Rule:** Hold a calm 4-beat pause after every question before inviting responses.  
> **Critical Thinking Strategies Activated:** `S-5`, `S-18`, `S-30`, `S-32`, `S-33` | **Intellectual Standards:** Accuracy, Precision, Relevance

1. Evaluating Evidence (S-33): In 'Horses Interlude', donkeys, sheep, pigs, and chickens showed up first—what exact sound and body clues proved each one was NOT a horse?
2. Testing Assumptions (S-30): Why might someone assume a donkey is a horse at first glance, and what closer observations separate the two?
3. Intellectual Humility (S-5): When we hear an unfamiliar animal sound behind a curtain, why is it wise to say 'Let's listen again and check clues' before deciding?

### Developmental Band Scaffolds (Ages 2–7)
- **Sprouts (Ages 2–4):** Sort animal voice clues aloud: 'Horse sound or different friend?'
- **Pathfinders (Ages 4–5):** Name two observable clues (ears, hooves, voice, size) that separate a horse from a donkey or sheep.
- **Wayfarers (Ages 6–7):** Explain why one shared trait (four legs) is not enough evidence to classify two creatures as identical.
